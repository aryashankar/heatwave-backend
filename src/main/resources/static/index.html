Understanding the Numbers

What WBGT and UTCI actually measure, and why the risk tiers are set where they are.

What we measure

Two heat-stress indices, not just air temperature.

WBGT (Wet-Bulb Globe Temperature) combines air temperature, humidity, wind, and solar radiation into a single number — it reflects how heat actually stresses the body far better than air temperature alone, and is the standard metric behind most occupational heat-safety guidelines worldwide.

We estimate it using the Australian Bureau of Meteorology's simplified outdoor formula, since a physical black-globe sensor isn't available. UTCI is a lighter complementary index, adjusted for wind and solar load, used alongside WBGT rather than in place of it.

Both are working approximations for early warning — not clinical-grade readings.

Why these thresholds

Adjusted for a heat-acclimatized population.

Generic WBGT thresholds are often calibrated for temperate climates and unacclimatized populations. People living in Indian cities are typically acclimatized to higher heat and humidity, and occupational heat-exposure research shows acclimatized individuals tolerate meaningfully higher WBGT before the same physiological strain sets in. We've raised our tier boundaries to reflect that:

Safebelow 30°C WBGT
Caution30–32°C
Danger32–34°C
Extreme Dangerabove 34°C

These are working estimates for an early-warning tool, not clinical thresholds — they'd benefit from refinement against local heat-illness health data.

FOR EVERY NEIGHBOR

Small actions can lower heat risk.

Pause outdoor work during peak hours, drink water regularly, and check in with someone who may need help — especially older neighbors, outdoor workers, and young children.

  • Reschedule strenuous outdoor work to early morning or evening.
  • Drink water regularly through the day, even before feeling thirsty.
  • Wear light, loose clothing and stay in shade when possible.
  • Check on elderly neighbors, outdoor workers, and young children.
  • Know the signs of heat exhaustion: dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating, weakness.
